# linear a tablet ht 31 and the two readings

HT 31 is a Linear A tablet from Hagia Triada. Published by Halbherr in the 1903 excavation reports and re-edited by Godart and Olivier in GORILA volume 1 (1976).

Nine lines. A libation formula, probably. The sign group JA-SA-SA-RA-ME opens line 1. It appears across at least nine Linear A tablets from four sites. Nobody knows what it means. The phonetic values are borrowed from Linear B and may not apply.

The tablet is 7.4 cm wide. Lines run left to right.

In GORILA the signs are transcribed in that order. 54 signs across nine lines.

Pugliese Carratelli's 1945 edition gives the same tablet with the lines read right to left. He does not justify this. A short footnote says only that the "ductus of certain signs" suggests the opposite direction. He transcribes 54 signs. The sign at position 27, dead center, is the same in both readings. It is sign AB 08, usually transliterated A.

Everything else differs. The two readings share no trisyllable.

I checked both against Ellerbe's 1968 list of recurrent Linear A sign groups. The GORILA reading matches four known groups. The Pugliese Carratelli reading matches five. One of the five, on his line 6, is KU-RO, the Linear A word for "total." It does not appear anywhere in the GORILA reading of the same tablet.

Both scholars worked from the original. The tablet is in Heraklion, case 7.

The GORILA facsimile and the Pugliese Carratelli facsimile are both drawn from autopsy. I laid them side by side on the desk. They are not mirror images. They are different drawings of the same object. Some signs are present in one and absent in the other. I counted the signs in each facsimile. GORILA: 54. Pugliese Carratelli: 54.

I counted them again. GORILA: 53. The sign at the end of line 4 in GORILA is a small vertical stroke that Godart and Olivier mark with a query. On the second count I did not include it.

Pugliese Carratelli does not have anything at that position. He has a stroke two signs earlier that GORILA does not show.

The central sign, position 27, is unambiguous in both. A single horizontal with a dot above it. Pugliese Carratelli draws the dot slightly left of the stroke's midpoint. Godart and Olivier draw it centered.

HT 31 has been photographed four times since 1903. The 1903 plate shows the dot left of center. The 1952, 1976, and 2004 photographs show it centered. The tablet has not been conserved in the interval between 1903 and 1952 according to the museum's records. The records for that period are incomplete.

Ellerbe's notes, which I have been reading in the microfilm copy at the university, mention HT 31 once. The entry is three words. "Count both ways."
