# the indus sign that reverses on the reverse

Indus seal M-1186, Mohenjo-daro, excavated 1927, now in the National Museum of India.

Steatite. 28 x 28 mm. Six signs across the top register, a unicorn below, a feeding trough beneath the unicorn's head. Standard.

The six signs, obverse, left to right in Mahadevan's 1977 reading: 342, 099, 176, 405, 267, 051.

The seal is a seal. Seals produce impressions. The impression reverses the image. This is what seals do.

Parpola's 1994 catalogue gives the impression reading as: 051, 267, 405, 176, 099, 342. Reversed, as expected.

Wells's 2015 sign concordance gives the impression reading as: 051, 267, 405, 176, 342, 099.

The last two signs swap. Not reversed. Reordered.

I checked this three times. Parpola reads the impression right to left, straightforward mirror. Wells reads the impression right to left for the first four signs and then left to right for the last two. He does not flag this. It is presented as the reading.

Wells's footnote on M-1186 reads: "sign 342 takes the direction of the reader, not of the row." I do not know what this means. The footnote has no reference and no elaboration.

Sign 342 in Mahadevan is a bent bracket with a dot inside the bend. Wells does not illustrate it in the body of the concordance. He illustrates it in appendix B, plate 7, where it appears four times at the center of a page otherwise blank.

I pulled the CISI photograph of M-1186 obverse and the CISI photograph of a modern impression taken in 1988. On the obverse, sign 342 is oriented with the bracket opening left. On the impression, the bracket opens left as well. It has not reversed.

The other five signs have reversed. 342 has not.

I counted the signs on both images. Six and six.

I counted them again. On the impression, between signs 099 and 342, there is a faint vertical stroke I had not seen on the obverse. It is not in Mahadevan. It is not in Parpola. It is not in Wells. It is present on the 1988 impression and absent on the 1927 obverse photograph.

The NMI online catalogue entry for M-1186 was last updated in March 2009. The record shows seven signs on the impression and six on the obverse. No one has corrected this.

The seal is not available for study. The entry notes it is on loan. The borrowing institution is not listed.
